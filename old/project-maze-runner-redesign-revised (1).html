Maze Runner Trilogy Redesign

Book cover redesigns exploring typography, layout, and a cohesive visual language across a trilogy.

Discipline
Print design
Focus
Typography, layout, book design

Overview.

This project reimagines the covers and book design for the Maze Runner trilogy. The goal was to explore how typography, composition, and repeated visual elements could work together to create a connected identity across multiple books while allowing each piece to function as part of a larger set.

Through sketching, layout exploration, and refinement, I developed the design from early concepts into a physical book format. The process gave me the opportunity to consider how a visual system carries across front covers, back covers, and the overall presentation of a series.

Design
Direction.

The initial design direction brought together early compositional ideas and established a starting point for the wider redesign. This cover concept introduces the book format and sets the tone for the layouts that follow.

Process:
Sketches.

I began by exploring ideas on paper before developing selected directions digitally. These process materials document the early stages of the project, when I tested possibilities, considered different compositions, and worked out how the design might translate to the page.

Process:
Layout.

After exploring the initial ideas, I refined the composition and considered how the elements worked together across the book design. These layout views show the project moving from experimentation toward a more consistent and resolved presentation.

Final
Covers.

The final stage brought the design into a physical format. Seeing the front and back covers together makes it possible to consider the work as a complete object, rather than as an isolated graphic. The finished presentation brings the typography and layout decisions into a tangible book design.

Reflection.

This project allowed me to explore the relationship between typography, layout, and physical print design through a multi-part book redesign. Moving from early sketches to refined layouts and a finished object helped me consider both individual compositions and how they work together as a consistent series. It also reinforced the value of testing ideas, refining details, and evaluating a design in its intended format.
