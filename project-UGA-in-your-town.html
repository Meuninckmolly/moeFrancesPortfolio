Traveling Exhibition · Campaign Design

UGA Athletics in
Your Town.

A collection of print and digital design assets supporting the University of Georgia's summer traveling exhibition.

Project
UGA in Your Town
Deliverables
Signage, bifold advertising, social media, and website assets
Focus
Exhibition promotion and visual communication

Overview.

The Hargrett library was in need of digital and print graphics for their traveling exhibit “UGA Athletics in Your Town"

Exhibition
Signage.

The exhibit's signage showcases the visual system as it is translated across different sections of the exhibit while holding true to its primary identity.

Exhibition signage

Process:
Sketches.

Utilizing the information gathered in my research I explored a plethora of different directions, Soviet Propaganda, Geometric imagery, American Propaganda, and photomontage. I sketched all of my ideas prior to taking my selected directions into Adobe to explore further.

Exploration

Process:
Iteration.

Taking inspiration from my previous sketches I made iterations of the primary graphic for the exhibit. Experimenting with Type, color, and imagery that followed the geometric propaganda seen in the soviet union. However, the overarching issue with these was the lack of overt American representation that was necessary for the final product.

Website assets

Process:
Signage.

Prior to starting work on text panels I gave myself some clear outlines, I forged a guide on color palette, font choices, and font sizing. I experimented with more simplistic, geometric visuals as well as more complex versions inspired by safety pamphlets on atomic protocols.

Text Panels & Infographics

Final
Signage.

I eventually landed on a more simplistic and straightforward design for the text panels to enhance the accessibility, structure, and general ominousness of the time period.

Text Panels & Infographics

Conclusion.

This project challenged me to translate years of history into graphical material that engaged with a cohesive visual system that was transformable into different platforms both print and digital. It forced me into a deeper state of understanding to achieve the ability to convey the complex emotions of the Cold war in an easily understood way. This project gave me experience leading a collaborative design process alongside staff and an assistant designer and allowed me to encounter communicating my design decisions and needs to ensure the success of the final outcome.
