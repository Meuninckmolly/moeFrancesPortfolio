The Frontlines of the Cold War

A cohesive graphic system for a history exhibition.

Discipline
Exhibition design
Focus
Visual systems, editorial, environmental graphics
Project
02 / 08
Discuss a project ↗

Overview.

The Russell Library was in need of digital and print graphics for its exhibit, “The Frontlines of the Cold War: Georgia, America, and a World Divided.” The client requested designs inspired by Cold War propaganda that created a strong and domineering feeling to convey the emotions felt during this time period. Each aspect needed to follow a strict system that was accessible and cohesive, ensuring visitors could easily navigate the exhibit without losing the visual identity throughout.

Exhibition
Signage.

The exhibit's signage showcases the visual system as it is translated across different sections of the exhibit while holding true to its primary identity.

Process:
Sketches.

Utilizing the information gathered in my research, I explored a plethora of different directions: Soviet propaganda, geometric imagery, American propaganda, and photomontage. I sketched all of my ideas prior to taking my selected directions into Adobe to explore further.

Process:
Iteration.

Taking inspiration from my previous sketches, I made iterations of the primary graphic for the exhibit, experimenting with type, color, and imagery that followed the geometric propaganda seen in the Soviet Union. However, the overarching issue with these was the lack of overt American representation that was necessary for the final product.

Process:
Signage.

Prior to starting work on text panels, I gave myself some clear outlines and forged a guide for color palette, font choices, and font sizing. I experimented with more simplistic, geometric visuals as well as more complex versions inspired by safety pamphlets on atomic protocols.

Final
Signage.

I eventually landed on a more simplistic and straightforward design for the text panels to enhance the accessibility, structure, and general ominousness of the time period.

Conclusion.

This project challenged me to translate years of history into graphical material that engaged with a cohesive visual system that was transformable across print and digital platforms. It pushed me to develop a deeper understanding of how to convey the complex emotions of the Cold War in an easily understood way. This project gave me experience leading a collaborative design process alongside staff and an assistant designer, and allowed me to practice communicating my design decisions and needs to ensure the success of the final outcome.
